# Home blood pressure: categories, thresholds and how to measure

*General knowledge, not findings about anyone. Guidelines change; check the date above.
Not medical advice.*

## 1. The same number can get different labels

Category systems differ between guideline bodies, and **home readings have different
thresholds than office readings.**

| Guideline | Category | Office | Home |
|---|---|---|---|
| AHA/ACC 2025 | Normal | <120 and <80 | not verified here |
| AHA/ACC 2025 | Elevated | 120 to 129 and <80 | not verified here |
| AHA/ACC 2025 | Stage 1 hypertension | 130 to 139 or 80 to 89 | not verified here |
| AHA/ACC 2025 | Stage 2 hypertension | ≥140 or ≥90 | not verified here |
| ESC 2024 | Elevated BP | 120/70 to <140/90 | 120/70 to <135/85 |
| ESC 2024 | Hypertension | ≥140/90 | ≥135/85 |
| ESH 2021 (home monitoring) | Hypertension | ≥140/90 | ≥135/85 |

All values in mmHg. **"Not verified here"** means the home equivalents in the AHA/ACC
2025 guideline were not read from the guideline itself for this file, so they are not
used. That is an honest gap, not an oversight
([METHOD.md § 6](../../METHOD.md#6-semantics-before-numbers)).

The AHA/ACC 2025 categories are unchanged from 2017, and the guideline recommends home
monitoring to confirm an office diagnosis [1]. ESC 2024 kept hypertension at office
≥140/90 and added the category *elevated BP* [2, 3]; the home column comes from a table in
a commentary by the Spanish Society of Cardiology's guideline working group, which
reproduces the guideline's thresholds by measurement method [4]. ESC 2024 recommends
out of office measurement for diagnosis because it detects white coat and masked
hypertension [5].

## 2. How home blood pressure is measured for a diagnosis

From the European Society of Hypertension's 2021 position paper on home monitoring [6]:

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Device | Clinically validated upper arm cuff; wrist and finger devices not generally recommended |
| Before measuring | 5 minutes seated rest |
| Readings | Two per occasion, 1 minute apart |
| Schedule | Morning and evening, for 7 days (not fewer than 3) |
| Threshold | Average ≥135/85 mmHg indicates hypertension |
| First day | Whether to discard it is debated; it matters most for short schedules |

A pooled analysis of 2,122 people found first day home readings about **2.8/1.4 mmHg
higher** than the following days, with values settling after day 3 [7].

**Single readings taken at random moments are not what these thresholds were built
for.** The position paper's conditions (seated rest first, two readings, a fixed
schedule) exist because circumstances change a reading [6].

## 3. When a single reading is an emergency

The American Heart Association describes readings **higher than 180/120 mmHg** as severe
hypertension: wait at least a minute and measure again. If it stays that high, it needs
attention from a health care professional right away; with symptoms such as chest pain,
shortness of breath, weakness, or changes in vision or speech, call emergency services
[8].
